## Bigger than you think

China covers about 9.6 million square kilometres, roughly the size of the United States or the whole of Europe. From the western edge of Xinjiang to the east coast is more than 4,000 kilometres, yet the whole country runs on a single time zone: Beijing time. On a winter morning in Kashgar, the sun doesn't rise until nearly ten o'clock.

## A land of three giant steps

Picture China as a staircase falling from west to east. The top step is the Tibetan Plateau, much of it higher than 4,000 metres. The middle step holds deserts, basins and plateaus, from the Gobi to the hills of Yunnan. The lowest step is the green, crowded east, where most cities and farmland lie below 500 metres. The great rivers, the Yangtze and the Yellow River, tumble down these steps on their way to the sea.

## Every climate in one country

In January you can stand in −25°C at Harbin's ice festival while people swim off the beaches of Hainan. Most of China has hot, rainy summers and dry winters, so the best season depends entirely on where you go.

> In one trip you can eat hand-pulled noodles in a desert oasis and rice noodles beside a rainforest.

## Fifty-six peoples

Most people in China are Han Chinese, but 55 other ethnic groups live here too, many of them in the borderlands: Uyghur and Kazakh communities in Xinjiang, Tibetans on the plateau, and dozens of peoples in Yunnan alone, each with their own food, festivals and way of building a home.

:::tip
**Planning tip:** distances are huge, but China has the world's largest high-speed rail network and plenty of domestic flights. Choose two or three regions and give each one time, rather than trying to see everything at once.
:::

## Where our stories begin

We start with three very different corners of the country: **Heilongjiang**, where winter turns cities into ice; **Yunnan**, a land of mountains, tea and many peoples; and **Fujian**, where medieval sea trade left temples, mosques and giant round earthen houses.
